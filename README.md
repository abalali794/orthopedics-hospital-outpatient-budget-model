
Executive Summary
Orthopedics Service Line Budget & Performance Model (HOPD)
=====================================================================

This project is a driver-based Orthopedics HOPD operating budget model that connects
volume → net revenue (via payor mix/yield) → cost structure → profitability, and extends
the budget into procedure-level margins, OR/clinic capacity, break-even risk, and
workforce productivity KPIs. All key assumptions (volumes, charges, net-to-gross,
implants/supplies, overhead allocations, staffing, and OR time/turnover) roll through
automatically to outputs.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
FY2026 Budget (vs FY2025 Actual) highlights
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Net revenue: $54.4M (+1.4%) driven by +3.1% surgical cases, partially offset by -4.8%
clinic visits

Total expenses: $52.7M (+1.8%) with the main pressure from medical supplies (+10.9%,
+$1.0M)

NOI: $1.75M (down $0.14M) and operating margin 3.21% (down 0.31 pp)


---------------------------------------------------------------------
Where profitability is made (procedure-level view)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Profit is concentrated in a few procedures: Rotator cuff repair (57.9% fully-loaded
margin), ACL reconstruction (54.7%), meniscus repair (32.8%), and arthroscopy (29.0%).
Meanwhile, fracture repair (-48.2%) and carpal tunnel release (-17.0%) are loss-making
after facility allocations, highlighting where pricing, cost, site-of-service, or
allocation methodology must be reviewed.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
Capacity constraint (operational feasibility)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The model shows the OR is the limiting constraint: 4,923 OR hours required vs 4,500
available (109% utilization), an annual gap of 423 hours. In contrast, the clinic has
headroom (94% utilization; ~1,520 visits of slack). This indicates surgical growth
requires additional blocks and/or efficiency improvements (turnover + case time), while
clinic growth can be absorbed without new capacity.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
Revenue quality & payor risk
---------------------------------------------------------------------

A blended net-to-gross of 31.7% combined with a heavy reliance on commercial yield
(Private: 53% mix × 44% NTG) makes profitability sensitive to payor mix shifts and
contract rate changes. This framework allows quick scenario testing of reimbursement/
yield changes.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
Break-even risk framing
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Overall service line break-even net revenue is $46.8M vs budget $54.4M, providing a
cushion; however, a surgery-only break-even calculation suggests that under current
allocations, surgery would require materially higher volume to cover fixed costs—
flagging either a true structural issue or the need to refine cost allocation logic.
